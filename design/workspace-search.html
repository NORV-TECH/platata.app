Workspace Search

One box on the Workspace that finds settings, menu, clients, team, sales and suppliers as you type, grouped by kind, and lets you change a setting right in the results. Every phone below is live: type, tap the chips, flip the switches.

Built on the Workspace screen and the Burger House demo data in this repo. Setting names, taxes and printers are placeholders until we map them to the app's real settings.

How search decides

The rules the prototype above runs on. They're simple on purpose, so they're easy to build and easy to predict.

  1. Every word you type must match the start of a word. Case and accents don't matter: andres finds Andrés, ham pol finds Hamburguesa de Pollo. With 3+ letters it also matches inside a word.
  2. More than names are searched. Each setting has aliases (dark mode → Theme, receipt printer → Registers & Printers, the Spanish and Portuguese names). Records match on secondary fields: a client's phone, an order number, the employee, client and items on a sale, the options inside a modifier group.
  3. Scores. Exact name 100 · name starts with it 92 · words found 84 down to 76 (earlier is better) · alias 6 less than the same on the name · keyword 55 · secondary field 45. Sales count ×0.85, suppliers ×0.9, reports ×0.8 so they never bury settings or people. Frequently used things get up to +2.5.
  4. Best match only when there's a clear winner: the top result scores 85 or more and leads the next one by 6 or more. Two clients called Andrés tie, so nothing is picked for you.
  5. Sections. Create actions come first (at most 2). Then sections by their best score, in steps of 10; ties use one fixed order: taxes, payment methods, printers, modifier groups, settings, items, categories, combos, clients, team, sales, suppliers, reports. Short configured lists (taxes, payment methods, printers) keep their own order. In All, 3 rows per section and "See all N".
  6. Chips are scopes. With a query they show only the kinds that have results, with counts. Tapping one shows everything of that kind, with no 3-row cap.
  7. Settings act in place. Switches and choices change the setting from the result row. Every setting shows its path (Workspace › Tax) so people learn where it lives, and "Browse all settings" is always one tap away.
  8. Never a dead end. With no results: the closest matches using the first 3 letters of each word, "New item …" with what was typed, and the way back to browsing.
  9. Speed (proposal). Settings, menu, clients and team live on the device and update on every keystroke. Sales search the server about 250 ms after typing stops, showing today's sales from the device first.
